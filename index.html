Kita
Your Smart Wallet & AI Financial Companion

Fintech · Financial Inclusion UX

Project Overview

Kita [Filipino: "earnings" / "to see"] is a smart wallet paired with an AI financial companion that helps users save, manage, and understand their money through personalized insights and guidance — while keeping them fully in control of every financial decision.

It's built for Filipino informal and gig workers — freelancers, social media sellers, ride-hailing drivers, and anyone with irregular income — whose earnings are real but rarely tracked, saved, or understood in a way that leads to action. Most finance apps tell people what already happened: a transaction log, a static balance. Few help them answer the questions that actually matter — "Can I afford this?" "How much should I save this week?" "Will I have enough for rent?" "Why am I always running out of money?" Kita's AI companion is designed to meet users at that question, turning raw earnings data into timely, personalized guidance instead of just charts.

I designed and built Kita end-to-end — from concept and user research through UX/UI design to a working front-end and back-end implementation — as a self-initiated product exploring financial inclusion for the Filipino workforce, aligned with BSP's digital finance goals.

Design Process

🔍 Understanding the Informal Worker's Money Habits


I started by mapping out who Kita is actually for: someone who earns from freelancing, sells on social media, drives for ride-hailing apps, or otherwise lives on irregular income. I looked at what they already use to manage money today — physical ledgers, GCash, cash set aside at home — and where those tools fall short.


Key insights:


• Income is irregular, so budgeting apps built around a fixed monthly salary don't fit how these users actually earn


• Existing apps are good at telling users what already happened, but few help them decide what to do next — "Can I afford this?", "How much should I save this week?", "Will I have enough for rent?"


• Trust in formal financial institutions is low, so guidance needed to feel personal and low-risk rather than corporate



🧭 Defining the AI-First Core Loop


Rather than compete with established e-wallets on payments alone, I made the AI companion the hero of the product — the thing that gives ordinary Filipinos something they could never normally afford: a personal financial advisor. I scoped Kita around four focused pillars: smart wallet, savings goals, AI financial companion, and personalized financial insights — deliberately keeping the MVP buildable rather than a full marketplace.


🔹 What makes the AI companion useful is that it's actionable, not just descriptive. For example:


• "Your electricity bill is due in 6 days. Based on your recent income, setting aside ₱250 today would keep you on track."


• "You spent 18% more on food this month, but you're still on pace to reach your emergency fund."


🔹 Other key UX decisions:


• Manual + quick-log earnings entry, designed for one-handed use on the go


• Goal-based savings pockets tied to real needs — tuition, emergency fund, fare


• In-app-only peer transfers to keep the MVP scoped and compliant

MAPS
Math Academic Progress-tracking System

EdTech · Academic Platform Design · Korean Hagwon Management

Project Overview

MAPS, the Math Academic Progress-tracking System, is the operational backbone for teachers, directors, and administrators at Korean private math academies (학원 / hagwon). It handles lesson planning, student progress tracking through the proprietary VP (Value Point) system, and the monthly reports sent directly to parents.

The existing MVP had been designed and built as a forced standalone system, treated as its own separate application even though it was never meant to run independently of HEMS (Hybrid Education Management System), the platform it was always intended to be part of. This, combined with a feature-first build process that skipped UX planning, left the product with usability debts that could no longer be resolved through incremental fixes. When I was assigned on December 15, 2025, the brief was to redesign MAPS as a single-page (one-pager) module, integrated into HEMS as one of its menus, without being constrained by what already existed.

Details

  • RoleUX Design Lead
  • IndustryEducation / EdTech · Hagwon Management
  • PlatformWeb Application
  • TimelineDec 2025 – Mar 2026
  • TeamW Soft Labs
  • ToolsFigma, Materio UI Kit, Claude, Obisidian

Design Process

🔍 Getting to the Root of the Problem


Before touching a single wireframe, I requested access to all MAPS files and documentation on December 12, 2025, and conducted a full UX audit of the live product. By that point, MAPS was already running a beta across 15 academies, with 150 instructors actively using the system to manage and track progress for over 3,000 enrolled students — so the stakes of getting the UX right were real, not hypothetical. Combined with five rounds of real teacher and director feedback collected via Confluence from November through December, plus a formal Beta Testing UX Analysis covering 23 identified UI/UX issues across the November 6 to 19 beta period, a clear failure pattern emerged.


The beta testing period drew on direct input from 7+ users across the system's three roles: 1 CEO/leadership stakeholder in a dedicated strategic feedback session, 1 academy director across two separate feedback sessions, and 5+ teaching staff through written feedback and verbal interviews, validated against a technical feasibility session with the development team. This qualitative sample sat against the backdrop of the full beta deployment, spanning 15 academies, 150 instructors, and over 3,000 enrolled students under management, giving the feedback real operational weight.


Users weren't failing because the system was complex. They were failing because the system was designed for the database, not for the person in front of it.


The 23 issues clustered into five themes, weighted by severity. Information Architecture Mismatch was rated Critical severity and flagged directly by CEO-level strategic feedback as the highest-impact gap. Interface Localization (5 of the 23 issues, 22%) and Input Interaction Friction (4 of the 23 issues, 17%) were both rated High severity and affected daily operations across every user group. Visual Design and Readability accounted for 2 of the 23 issues (9%) at Medium-High severity, largely tied to PDF report quality. Workflow Flexibility and Control, covering the inability to edit or delete entries, was rated High severity and cited as a daily blocker by teaching staff specifically.



Our audit and beta testing showed:


• Too many clicks for basic tasks. Creating a learning plan required 10+ clicks through multiple pages; entering test scores required navigating deep into student profiles


• VP assignment was opaque. Teachers couldn't see why numbers looked different than expected


• Navigation was deeply nested with no consistent hierarchy across roles


• Directors had a completely broken approval queue. Plans submitted by instructors simply didn't appear, forcing manual KakaoTalk follow-ups


• HQ had no cross-academy visibility. The System Analytics Dashboard displayed "Failed to load analytics" with no fallback


• No design system. Inconsistent patterns across every page


• Korean localization was missing across critical UI elements, creating daily cognitive friction for the entire user base


• No ability to edit or delete entries after creation. Teachers had to contact admins to fix even simple mistakes



The beta analysis identified a fundamental conceptual model gap: the system was built as a data entry tool, but users needed a diagnostic decision-support dashboard. That single insight became the organizing principle of V2.





👥 User Personas


Research and beta testing produced three distinct user personas, each with documented pain points and clear design implications.


Jimin Park (박지민) — The Dedicated Teacher
Age 28, full-time math instructor managing 30 students across 4 class levels. Logs into MAPS 2 to 3 times daily. Spends her entire Monday off finishing monthly reports manually because the system offers no automation. Keeps a personal Excel spreadsheet as backup because she doesn't trust the data.


Pain points: 10+ clicks to create a learning plan, no student search box, no duplicate book detection, no approval status visibility, and monthly reports that require manually copying data from three separate sections.



Sunho Kim (김선호) — The Busy Approver
Age 42, Academy Director overseeing 3 instructors and 12 students. Prefers iPad. Arrives each afternoon to an approval queue that shows empty despite instructors having submitted plans, forcing him to follow up via KakaoTalk. When the queue works, approving 8 plans one by one takes 30 to 40 minutes.


Pain points: Broken approval queue, inaccurate dashboard metrics, no bulk approve, and a system that makes him feel like an approval button rather than a leader.



Hyejin Lee (이혜진) — The System Guardian
Age 35, HQ Operations Manager across 10 academy locations and 28 instructors. Highly technical. Starts every day by opening the System Analytics Dashboard, which has been showing a failed load error for 3 days straight.


Pain points: No executive reporting capability, test data polluting all metrics, no cross-academy search or comparison, and zero visibility into system usage patterns.



🔬 Research & Competitive Analysis


Research was conducted across three comparable platforms to benchmark MAPS against existing solutions in the Korean education market, alongside three UX case studies of Korean academic management systems. Research focus was narrowed per PM's direction: prioritize clarity for non-technical users, meaning teachers, directors, and HQ operators.



Canvas LMS, Global LMS adapted for Korean institutions, is feature-rich and responsive but too complex for hagwon use. Teachers need lightweight grade entry, not a university-level LMS. Takeaway: stay task-focused and avoid feature bloat.


클래스팅 (Classting), Korean K-12 and hagwon platform, has clean Material Design, strong mobile responsiveness, and intuitive color-coded performance indicators, but lacks math-specific diagnostic depth and requires 4+ clicks for common tasks. Takeaway: differentiate through diagnostic depth and fewer clicks.


매쓰홀릭 (Matholic), Math-specialized diagnostic platform, leads the market in math-specific diagnostics and curriculum alignment but carries a dated, desktop-only interface with overwhelming information density. Takeaway: combine Matholic's diagnostic depth with a modern, clean UI.



Case Study Research: Korean Academic Management Systems


SNU Genie (Seoul National University) showed that legacy systems can be transformed by reorganizing around user needs instead of admin structure. Lesson: role-based dashboards should reflect how users think, not how the database is organized.


Token (Secondary School Disciplinary System) showed empathy can be designed into even bureaucratic interfaces. Lesson: design for understanding, not just recording.


Hagwon Management Systems — Cost of Poor UX confirmed the stakes: when official platforms fail, schools migrate to consumer apps like Band and ClassDojo instead. Lesson: if V2 doesn't solve real friction, it gets abandoned for whatever's easier.





🧭 Designing for Role, Not for Feature


With research, beta findings, and stakeholder directives in hand, I led the full IA-through-prototype pipeline across all three user roles.



Key design decisions:


• Role-based navigation architecture — Instructors get a horizontal 6-tab bar (VP Progress, Test Scores, Learning Attitude, Makeup Sessions, Evaluation, Performance Prep) for quick student data switching; Directors get a bottom tab navigation tuned to approval workflows; HQ Operators get three tab groups — Management, Performance, and Monitoring — plus bottom navigation for broader area access


• Diagnostic dashboard, not data entry table — The core paradigm shift from the beta analysis. The student view was redesigned from an alphabetical list to a problem-first display: Normal / Needs Attention / Critical status overview at the top, with quick filters by Homework / Progress / Test issues, and student cards showing progress bars, trend indicators (↑ → ↓), and last-updated timestamps. Time to identify a problem student: under 30 seconds versus 10+ minutes in the current system


• VP system transparency — Real-time running VP counters, visible Book → Unit → Topic hierarchy, and inline breakdowns showing how learning group factors (HN, AC, ST, RS) affect the final calculation


• Bilingual Korean/English interface — A critical-priority finding from beta testing. Korean was deprioritized in the MVP, creating daily cognitive friction for every user. V2 introduces bilingual display patterns (e.g., AC - Achieve / 성취) across dropdowns and labels, Korean-primary buttons and actions, and fully Korean parent-facing monthly reports. English is the design-first language with Korean translation as the immediate next phase


• Edit and delete throughout — Beta testing found teachers were afraid to enter data incorrectly because mistakes required admin intervention to fix. V2 enables inline edit and delete with confirmation dialogs, session-level undo, and an audit log visible to Directors for transparency


• 3-level homework input — Replacing the binary checkbox (done / not done) with a three-state radio system: Complete (≥70%), Partial (30–70%), and Incomplete (<30% — the default). Color-coded green/yellow/red. Auto-saves on selection with no submit button required. Estimated 75% reduction in homework update time


• Student search and batch operations — Type-ahead search with fuzzy matching, filter by grade and group, and recent students shortcut — replacing the scroll-through-100-names workflow that beta users cited as a daily friction point. Batch test score entry for entering one test across multiple students simultaneously


• Icons over text wherever possible — Per PM direction, reducing localization burden and improving scannability across both language contexts


• Modular for HEMS integration — Since MAPS will eventually become a single "Progress Tracking" tab within HEMS, every component — tabs, data tables, student cards — was built to fold cleanly into the broader HEMS system without major rework



🖌️ Design Execution


Information Architecture


Information Architecture came first, establishing the full navigation hierarchy and menu structure across all three roles before any flows or screens were considered. The existing MVP had no coherent IA — menus were deeply nested, role boundaries were unclear, and there was no logical grouping of related functions. The V2 IA restructured everything from the ground up, defining what each role could access, how features were grouped, and how the three role contexts related to each other within the same system. This became the foundation every subsequent design decision was built on.



User Journey Maps and User Flows


With the architecture established, User Journey Maps and User Flows were developed for each role to understand how users move through the system to complete their core tasks. Journey maps captured the full experience across touchpoints, including the emotional lows (waiting for approval with no status feedback, spending an entire day off on reports) alongside the functional steps. User flows then translated those journeys into precise task paths: how a teacher creates and submits a learning plan, how a director processes an approval queue, how HQ pulls a system-wide report. These flows surfaced edge cases and decision points that would not have been visible from the IA alone, and directly shaped how screens were sequenced in wireframes.



Low-Fidelity Wireframes


Low-fidelity wireframes were completed next across all three roles, one dedicated Figma page per role. Wireframes established layout logic, navigation hierarchy, and content priority without introducing any visual decisions that might distract from structural feedback. This gave stakeholders a clear skeleton to react to: where things lived, how tabs were organized, what appeared above the fold, and how data was grouped on each screen. The Instructor, Director, and HQ wireframe pages each went through review before the work advanced to high fidelity.



Design System and Localization


The type system pairs Inter for all UI elements with Pretendard for Korean-language content. Pretendard was chosen specifically for its performance with Korean characters at small sizes, which matters when student names, academy names (영통관, 천천관), and group labels appear inside dense data tables. All table cells were tested with short Korean names (김) and long compound names (15+ characters) to ensure graceful truncation with tooltip fallback.


The Materio UI Kit was adopted as the main design standard for this redesign, chosen to keep MAPS visually and structurally uniform with the design system already developed for the existing HEMS app. Components, spacing conventions, and interaction patterns were built on top of Materio's foundation so that once MAPS is integrated as a HEMS menu item, it reads as a native part of the system rather than a bolted-on module.


The color system uses MAPS Yellow and MATH JKL Blue as brand primaries, MATH JKL being the hagwon business's own brand identity, with a semantic layer of Success Green, Warning Yellow, and Error Red for all status indicators across student cards, approval queues, and VP progress displays. The 4px base grid and 12-column layout at 1440px held consistently across all three role contexts, with the design system page serving as the single source of truth for spacing, component usage, and color application.



Component Architecture


The file uses a componentized structure built on Materio (Material Design Figma UI Kit) extended with a custom design system page. Core reusable components include Instructor Tabs, Director Tabs, HQ Operations Tabs, a Data Table component, and a Student Card component, each with defined properties and variants. Componentization was a deliberate call given the three-role surface area: any change to a shared element like the student record or data table needed to propagate consistently without manual updates across 16 Figma pages.


This modularity also directly serves the HEMS integration constraint. Since all MAPS functionality will eventually live inside a single HEMS menu tab, components needed to be portable and self-contained rather than tightly coupled to MAPS-specific layouts.



High-Fidelity Screens and Screen-Level Decisions


The Instructor view centers on a horizontal 6-tab bar (VP Progress, Test Scores, Learning Attitude, Makeup Sessions, Evaluation, Performance Prep) placed at the top of the screen. This reflects how instructors actually work: they move laterally across data types for the same student, not vertically through menus. Each tab surface is self-contained, with student context persistent across tab switches.


The Director dashboard was rebuilt around three zones: Action Required summary cards at the top (Pending Approvals, Delayed, VP Alert, Total Students), a Teacher VP Progress monitoring table in the middle showing Achieved VP, Required VP, rate, and On Pace or Delayed status per instructor, and the Learning Plan Approval queue at the bottom with All, Pending, Approved, and Rejected tabs plus inline Approve, Reject, Comment, and View actions. The goal was to give a Director everything needed to run a Monday morning approval session without leaving a single screen.


The HQ view uses three tab groups: Management (Dashboard, Users, Academies, Book Management, Student Group Management, VP Config), Performance (Instructor Performance, Academy Performance, System-Wide Progress), and Monitoring (Learning Attitude Evaluation, Test Score Entry, Makeup Sessions, System Reports), plus a bottom tab bar for broader navigation. This structure mirrors how HQ actually divides its responsibilities — administration, analytics, and operational oversight are distinct workstreams and the navigation reflects that.


Authentication flows were designed as a complete standalone system for V2, covering Register, Login, Verify Email, Two Steps, Forgot Password, and Reset Password, each with full variant states. These are temporary until HEMS single sign-on integration is built, but were designed with that migration in mind.



AI-Powered Usability Testing


To validate the high-fidelity screens before handoff, I ran AI-powered usability testing using Attention Insight across the Instructor, Director, and HQ dashboards. Averaged across all three, the design achieved a 58% Clarity Score, landing solidly within the Optimal clarity range (57–94%), with CTA visibility on the strongest-performing dashboard reaching 3.9% — above the 3.4% platform benchmark. Attention and Focus Maps consistently showed concentrated, purposeful focus on key content areas — student data, approval queues, and progress metrics — rather than scattered attention, confirming the layouts were guiding users where intended.


The tool also flagged a handful of minor refinements ahead of handoff — sharpening CTA prominence with sticky, high-contrast styling, tightening whitespace to surface more content above the fold, and promoting filters and pending-approval actions for faster access. These were incorporated as a final polish pass on top of an already solid foundation.











Outcome


🖼️ Design Status


All three roles have complete High Fidelity designs with interactive prototypes, built as a single-page module that will be integrated into HEMS as one of its role-based sidebar menu items, rather than accessed as a separate standalone application. The beta testing analysis identified 23 UI/UX issues across five themes: localization gap, information architecture mismatch, input interaction friction, visual design and readability, and workflow flexibility. V2 addresses all critical and high-priority items in the design.



Beta tester and stakeholder feedback reflected in the design:


• Teachers in beta testing spent 4–5 hours on monthly reports due to manual data copying. V2's auto-generated report drafts with pre-filled test scores, VP data, and evaluation entries eliminate that manual step entirely


• Directors reported a broken approval queue as their most critical issue. The V2 Director dashboard rebuilds the queue from scratch, with bulk approve, accurate student and plan counts, and exception-only flagging for non-standard submissions


• HQ Operations was unable to perform core job functions due to a broken System Analytics Dashboard. V2 rebuilds this as the primary landing view for HQ, with academy comparison tables, real-time system health indicators, test data cleanup, and executive-ready export tools


• JKL Stakeholders expressed satisfaction with the completed IA, personas, journeys, and flows in a December 29 review — confirming design direction before proceeding to high fidelity



The MAPS V2 redesign demonstrates what happens when a complex, role-heavy system is rebuilt with the user's actual workflow as the organizing principle. One consolidated one-pager, accessed as a native menu item within HEMS's existing navigation, and a clear path from design-complete to development-ready.

Chong Hua Hospital
Chong Hua Experience App

Healthcare UX · Patient Experience Design

Project Overview

The Chong Hua Experience project focused on reimagining the patient journey across Chong Hua Hospital's digital and physical touchpoints. The goal was to reduce friction in appointment booking, wayfinding, and post-consultation follow-ups — creating a seamless, human-centered healthcare experience for patients and their families. Chong Hua Hospital, the second-largest hospital in Cebu and a leading tertiary care provider in the Visayas region, needed a comprehensive digital platform to support both patients and healthcare professionals.

As UX Lead at Yondu, I spearheaded the design of a dual-access portal focused on streamlining appointment scheduling, medical records access, and clinical coordination. What began as a proof-of-concept (POC) pitch rapidly evolved into a full-scale digital health transformation, winning approval from the hospital's Board of Directors and securing a P15M contract for design and development.

🔹 Patient Portal

• UI optimized for mobile with icon-based navigation for older users

🔹 Doctor Portal


• Manage schedules, access patient records, prescribe medications

🔹 Admin Portal

🔄 Future-Proofing: Nurse & Assistant Concepts

Although not yet in development, these concepts were shared internally to position the platform as modular and extensible.


Outcome

🚀 Soft Launch & Impact

The project resulted in a ₱15M contract win, a production-ready multi-role healthcare platform, and a modular foundation for future medical portals. Today, Chong Hua Hospital’s digital platform serves as a benchmark for user-centered healthcare innovation in the Philippines, unifying patients, doctors, and administrators within a single digital ecosystem to enable faster care delivery, improved communication, and a more efficient hospital experience.
